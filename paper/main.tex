\begin{abstract}
This diagnostic comment examines initial-state matching and pressure
realizability in arXiv:2607.07106v1. Its exact mixture identities are correct.
However, archived PIC and fluid initial moment-current profiles differ by
$9.87\%$ in the full-domain, $x$-averaged relative $\ell^2$ norm; local
Cartesian covariance pressures differ by about $20\%$ in $P_{xx}$ and
$38.4\%$ in $P_{zz}$. The PIC input specifies proper momentum where the
fluid input specifies ordinary velocity. Complete archive checks and
independent ten-moment layout validation corroborate the data audit.
Four clean-source fluid cases attempt comparisons of the posted and
aggregate-PIC-matched initializations, while distinguishing the unavailable
historical dirty build.
At identical one-fluid endpoint times, matching lowers $P_{xx}$ error against
PIC from $36.29\%$ to $28.85\%$, with smaller errors in density, current
and $P_{zz}$ as well. These are numerical endpoint results, not validated
physical dynamics: both one-fluid cases contain indefinite covariance
tensors at saved intermediate times, and the clean unmatched two-fluid
endpoint is also inadmissible. The matched two-fluid run terminates with an
invariant-check assertion before producing its comparison endpoint.
The clean implementation tests coordinate
pressures without testing full tensor positivity. The original archived
endpoints pass the same covariance checks; their intermediate trajectories
and exact executable remain unidentified. A conditional loader calculation,
an auxiliary homogeneous stability benchmark, a continuity clarification and
a cancellation-conditioned heat-flux bound provide separate diagnostics.
The results motivate matched moments and realizability checks without
disproving the source's exact algebra or establishing that its nonlinear
physical conclusions are false.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
Matching nominal drift expressions does not ensure matched moments between
a proper-momentum PIC initialization and an ordinary-velocity fluid
initialization. The archived
PIC moments and reloaded fluid output make that distinction quantitatively
testable. The full-profile and conditional finite-temperature checks
strengthen that initial-state finding. Clean-source evolutions quantify
numerical endpoint sensitivity, but saved covariance failures prevent
interpreting lower endpoint errors as validation of a physical trajectory.
The separate homogeneous benchmark shows why finite isotropization should
be calibrated as part of a closure; it establishes no unstable eigenmode
of the source's inhomogeneous sheet.
The exact moment decomposition remains valid and useful. Matching aggregate
moments, checking the complete pressure tensor, identifying the executed code,
and testing resolution are distinct requirements. The original archived
endpoint population tensors pass the covariance test; failures in the identified clean implementation do
not establish failures in the unavailable dirty build. Correcting the
density-transport explanation and conditioning relative-error statements
supports further comparison without declaring the source's nonlinear
physical conclusions disproved.
