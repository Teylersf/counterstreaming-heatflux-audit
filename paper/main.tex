\begin{abstract}
This diagnostic comment examines initial-state controls in arXiv:2607.07106v1
using the published decks, archived PIC moments and reloaded initial fluid
output. The central two-population moment identities are correct. However,
the PIC input specifies proper momentum where the fluid input specifies
ordinary velocity. The saved fluid states corroborate their initializers to
roundoff. Relative to either fluid model, the initial PIC moment-current
profile differs by $9.87\%$ in the full-domain, $x$-averaged relative
$\ell^2$ norm; the two lower-sheet central cells differ by about $11.6\%$.
Their Cartesian velocity-covariance pressures are about $20\%$ lower in
$p_{xx}$ and $38.4\%$ lower in $p_{zz}$. A conditional public-loader
calculation explains the finite-temperature drift reduction, while separating
verified archived moments from an unverified custom diagnostic implementation.
No native deposited-current file is identifiable in the complete posted PIC
inventory. Separately, an auxiliary homogeneous longitudinal benchmark has
a finite-isotropization fluid mode with growth $0.00952\omega_{pe}$ where
its collisionless kinetic reference is Penrose-stable. This is not a sheet
eigenanalysis or a refutation of the source's application-specific instability
claim. We also qualify the symmetry-plane density explanation and give a
cancellation-conditioned relative heat-flux bound. No new reconnection run
is claimed. A matched-state rerun is required to establish dynamical
significance; the source's later nonlinear conclusions are not disproved here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
Matching a nominal drift expression does not match a proper-momentum PIC
initialization to an ordinary-velocity fluid initialization. The archived
PIC moments and reloaded fluid output make that distinction quantitatively
testable. The full-profile and finite-temperature checks strengthen that
initial-state finding while leaving its nonlinear importance undetermined.
The separate homogeneous benchmark shows why finite isotropization should
be calibrated as part of a closure; it establishes no unstable eigenmode
of the source's inhomogeneous sheet.
The exact moment decomposition remains valid and useful. Correcting the
density-transport explanation and conditioning relative-error statements
provides a constructive basis for further comparison, without asserting
that the reported nonlinear reconnection results have been disproved.
